🌊 Pre-Production Reality: The Script Is Hyper-Fluid
The script is never static in pre-production. It continuously evolves and reshapes at three critical checkpoints: at the Create, at the Move, and at the Replacement. Embracing this fluidity rather than forcing premature lock-in is what drives video resonance and keeps the production pipeline frictionless.
✨ 1. At The Create (Ideation)

Initial brainstorm, rough narrative hooks, and P.A.R.A. framework outlines. Written in Obsidian and Canva notes as exploratory thoughts.

↔️ 2. At The Move (Placement & Tail Sandbox)

Dragging footage into Canva or the end of the timeline instantly reveals motion duration. Sentences are trimmed, condensed, or lengthened to fit the visual beats.

🔄 3. At The Replacement (Refinement & VO Inspector)

Swapping placeholder graphics for final motion loops and inspecting 1-sec screenshots. Voiceover is polished for booming delivery and locked to the 3 words/sec rule.

💡 How it accelerates production: Eliminates writer's block by removing the false expectation of a "perfect first draft." You let the visual footage guide the words, resulting in tighter synchronization and higher emotional punch.

⚡ Timeline Staging Tactic

End-of-Timeline Auditioning Sandbox (Tail Buffer)

When evaluating new footage, animations, or Canva visual candidates, drag them to the very end of the timeline (the buffer sandbox). Play and watch them in the native monitor first to check motion cadence before splicing them into the active story cut.

↔️ Open Horizontal Timeline
📥 1. Drag to Tail Zone
Drop incoming Google Flow MP4s or Canva stills well past the active 2:58 runtime mark. Keeps the primary edit completely safe.
▶️ 2. Native Playback Test
Watch the clip play in full screen and native framerate. Check loop smoothness, speed, and contrast against the UI palette.
✂️ 3. Trim & Lock In-Point
Find the best 2–4 second motion slice that matches the voiceover punchline (e.g. the cyan lightning bolt flash).
🎯 4. Slide into Locked Slot
Slide the vetted clip straight into its designated shot number on the main track, or mark it 🚫 Out of Scope if it doesn't fit.
📁 Asset Management Tactic

Canva Uploads Player & 'Used' Folder Hierarchy

Canva's upload drawer features a built-in video player and custom folder structure. Auditioning raw clips directly inside the upload drawer and maintaining a dedicated 📁 Used subfolder eliminates clutter and guarantees zero asset duplication across timeline cuts.

🎨 Canva Planning Deck ↗ 🖼️ Open Asset Gallery
▶️ 1. In-Drawer Media Player Preview
Hover or click video clips right inside Canva's upload sidebar to preview full MP4 loops with zero context switching.
📁 2. 'Used' Subfolder Pool Committed
Once a clip or graphic is placed into the video sequence, move it into the 📁 Used folder so the upload drawer only displays remaining candidate assets.
☁️ 3. Code & Azure Harmony Sync
Mirrors the ✅ Used & 🚫 Out of Scope tagging in the local Asset Gallery, keeping your Canva canvas, shotlist, and Azure Blob storage 100% synchronized.
🎬 Local Visual Rewrite Engine

1-Second Frame Extraction & Visual AI Voiceover Rewrite

Export your raw video draft with scratch voiceover into 3_Simulation/rawexport/. Run our local Python extractor to take frame screenshots every 1 second and generate an interactive rewrite document with AI-suggested variations tailored to the visual screen reality.

🌐 Open Visual Inspector ↗
📥 1. Drop Raw MP4 into Folder
Export video from Canva / Premiere with scratch audio into 3_Simulation/rawexport/raw_video.mp4.
⚡ 2. Run Local Python Extractor
Executes ffmpeg to extract 1 screenshot per second into 3_Simulation/rawexport/screenshots/.
🤖 3. Review 3x AI Suggested Takes
Inspects screenshots alongside ⚡ Punchy (165 WPM), 🧠 Strategic (140 WPM), and 🔥 Conversational (145 WPM) takes.
🎙️ 4. Lock Resonant Final Script
Write custom resonant takes directly in the markdown workbench or web inspector and copy back to research.html.

⏱️ The "3 Words a Second" Visual Rhythm Rule
In VO Inspector, we step second-by-second across the 200 frame screenshots and calibrate our voiceover script at an exact pace of ~2.5 to 3 words per second (150–180 WPM).
1 Sec Screen Time: ~3 words
e.g. "No more chaos."
2 Secs Screen Time: ~5–6 words
e.g. "I built an AI-native brain."
4 Secs Screen Time: ~10–12 words
e.g. "AI agents query and update my entire knowledge base."
